## Method

### 1. Universe (per market)
- Exclude:
  - CN: ST/*ST, delisting-period and suspended stocks, and stocks listed under 1 year.
  - HK/US: non-ordinary securities (ETFs, funds, warrants, preferreds, SPACs, OTC) and stocks listed under 1 year.
  - US (Phase 4): preferreds, notes, units and fractional depositary shares are dropped by listing name (ADRs, MLP common units and names that say ordinary/common shares stay); only the most traded share class per issuer (SEC CIK) is kept, e.g. GOOGL not GOOG.
  - HK (Phase 5): ordinary shares are Eastmoney company profiles with an organisation type (REITs, ETFs and other funds have none), without the RMB counters (8xxxx). Market value = all issued shares (H + A + unlisted domestic shares) × the HK price, as Eastmoney counts it. Privatised and merged names still in Eastmoney's list (Hang Seng Bank, Haitong, Dongfeng, ESR...) have no Yahoo prices and fall out at "traded on the last day".
- Liquidity: 20-day average daily turnover of at least ¥50m (CN), HK$20m (HK) or US$10m (US). These are config values.
- Keep the top 800 by total market value.
  - HK (Phase 5): only 413 of the 941 HK stocks listed a year or more pass HK$20m (2026-10-17), so the HK universe is ~410 names, not 800; 407 have complete data (dropped: 1 without a reporting currency from Yahoo, 3 with missing results, 2 with short price history).

### 2. Peer groups
Use the industry from the data source (the Eastmoney industry field in v1). Merge groups with fewer than 8 members into the nearest broader group, or compare them market-wide.
- HK (Phase 5): the Eastmoney HK industry (BELONG_INDUSTRY, ~30 Hang Seng-style groups); groups under 8 are compared market-wide. Banks, insurers and brokers are an exact list of sub-industries, the last level of Eastmoney's GICS-style HK industry type (config `financial_industries_hk`). HKEX, asset managers, lessors and consumer lenders are valued like other companies, as in CN and US.
- US (Phase 4): the Nasdaq screener industry; a non-financial industry with fewer than 8 members in the universe becomes "<Nasdaq sector> (other)". Banks, brokers and insurers are an exact list of Nasdaq industries (config `financial_industries_us`). "Specialty Insurers" is left out: in the US universe it holds the insurance brokers (Marsh, Aon, Gallagher, WTW, Brown & Brown), which the insurer method valued on goodwill-heavy books (Gallagher at -67%).

### 3. Metrics
Use the latest data, trailing 12 months where possible:
- price, market value, P/E, P/B, ROE
- revenue growth, net profit growth, gross margin
- debt/assets, operating cash flow ÷ net profit, dividend yield
- 12-month price return excluding the last month
- 1-year volatility

Winsorize each metric at the 2nd and 98th percentile within the market.
- Phase 7: cash conversion (operating cash flow ÷ net profit) is clipped to [−1, 3] and left out when the net margin is under 2% (config `[universe]`). It exploded on tiny profits and mixes consolidated cash flow with attributable profit: 91 US, 86 HK and 124 CN names were above 3 (兖煤澳大利亚 31.6 made a "top-30% quality" hold).

### 4. Fair value (per stock, in its quote currency)
- Banks, insurers and brokers: the P/B the peer group pays for that ROE (a cross-sectional fit of log P/B on ROE), plus the forward lens like other companies. Loss-making financials have no trailing value.
  - Changed in the Phase 5 review: until then this was blended 50/50 with the justified P/B = (ROE − g) / (r − g), and financials had no forward lens. That put Chinese insurers at +200-316% (中国人寿 H +238%, 中国太平 +316%, 中国人寿 A +192%), for two reasons. (1) It is an absolute anchor with one market-wide r, while each market prices its financials at its own implied cost of equity (Chinese banks ~13%, Chinese insurers ~20%, US banks ~8%), so whole-sector discounts became stock "upside": the median financial showed +42% in CN and +37% in HK, -12% in the US (peer-only: +9%, +10%, +3%). (2) It is a perpetuity on one year's ROE, and insurers' 32-37% TTM ROE is 2026 investment gains; analysts expect 16-28% next year. Now the largest insurer upsides are +44% (CN, 中国太保), +47% (HK, 中国人保), +56% (US, Everest), and 中国人寿 is +8% (A) / +4% (H).
- Other profitable companies: the P/B the peer group pays for similar ROE and growth (a fit of log P/B on ROE and growth within the group). Changed from P/E in the Phase 2 review: in A-shares the P/E fit gets a negative ROE coefficient in every group (low ROE usually means depressed earnings and a high P/E), which made cheap low-ROE stocks look undervalued.
- Loss-making companies: P/S fitted on gross margin and growth. If no fit is possible, the stock has no fair value and is excluded.
- Missing input (changed 2026-10-19, your call: a method gap is not a risk signal): a stock neither lens values because an input is missing gets a simpler peer multiple, marked 低信心: loss-makers without a gross profit line (biotech, telecom equipment, media, exchanges) on P/S and growth; profitable companies with negative book value and under 3 analysts on P/E and growth. Not when the stock is priced outside its peers' 5th-95th percentile spread around the fit: then its price rests on something else (pre-revenue Roivant at ~1,000x sales, AST SpaceMobile; 碧桂园's restructuring gain on negative book) and it stays without a value rather than getting a meaningless one. Loss-making financials still have no value.
- Negative book value (added in the Phase 4 review): P/B means nothing, so a profitable company with negative equity (mostly US buyback stocks: ABBV, MCD, BKNG, HLT, LOW, PM...) is valued on the forward lens alone, at normal conviction, and has no ROE (quality comes from margin, cash conversion and leverage). Without 3 analysts it has no fair value. Before, these were dropped: 35 of the US top 800. No CN stock is affected today.
- Forward lens (added in the Phase 2 review, because trailing numbers make one-off profit spikes look cheap and early fast growth look expensive): for non-financials with at least 3 analysts, also fit log forward P/E (price ÷ next year's consensus EPS) on expected EPS growth (2-year consensus CAGR) within the peer group. Fair value mid = 50/50 trailing and forward value (trailing only when there is no forward lens); the range always covers both. If the two lenses point opposite ways versus the price and differ by more than 50%, mark 低信心. Consensus EPS only; analysts' target prices and ratings are never used.
- HK forward lens (Phase 5): Yahoo has consensus EPS for this and next fiscal year only, so expected EPS growth is the 2-year CAGR from last year's actual EPS (Yahoo's own, often adjusted) to next year's consensus; forward P/E is on next year's consensus, as in CN and US.
- Fair value mid = fitted multiple × the company's own metric.
- Fair value low/high = from the 25th/75th percentile of the group's fit residuals.
- r = 10-year government bond yield + equity premium (CN 6%, HK 6%, US 5%), a config value. It is only used by the residual-income cross-check.
- Absolute cross-check: a simple residual-income value at the same r (excess ROE fades linearly to r over 10 years), kept in the picks CSV for reference. It does not set 低信心 (Phase 2 review: at r ≈ 7.7% the RI value is typically ~1/2.7 of what peers pay, so it flagged 44–79% of the market, including real growth stocks; the forward-lens disagreement is the 低信心 signal instead).
- Upside = fair value mid / price − 1.

### 5. Scores (sector-neutral z-scores within each market)
- Value: upside, earnings yield, book yield (for financials)
- Quality: ROE, gross margin, cash conversion, low leverage
- Growth: revenue and profit growth
- Momentum: 12-month return excluding the last month
- Risk: low volatility

Composite = 0.35 value + 0.25 quality + 0.15 growth + 0.15 momentum + 0.10 risk. The weights live in config.toml.
- Phase 7: each component is standardized within financials and within the rest before the market-wide z-score. Financial quality is ROE alone and the others average four metrics, so financials' quality scores spread wider and crowded the top (spread financials vs rest: US 1.55 vs 0.84, HK 1.29 vs 0.92, CN 1.35 vs 0.94; now ~1.0 on both sides).

### 6. Buckets (per market, always 5 each)
- 买入 BUY: upside ≥ 15%, quality ≥ peer-group median and momentum z ≥ −0.5. Take the top 5 by composite score.
- 持有 HOLD: quality in the market's top 30% and upside between −10% and +15%. Take the top 5 by quality score. These are good companies priced about right.
- 卖出 SELL: upside ≤ −15%, plus at least one of: quality in the bottom 40%, momentum z ≤ −0.5, or falling profits. The stock must be among the market's 400 largest. Take the 5 with the lowest composite score.
- Phase 7: the labels were 持有/不追高 and 卖出/回避 until then (valuation v2 drops the qualifiers). The report takes the label from the bucket, so older picks CSVs show the new names too.
- Phase 7: buy and hold candidates that fail one-off check 1 or 2 (Method 8) take no place; see Method 8.
- At most 2 stocks per peer group per bucket, and each stock in only one bucket.
- One issuer appears only once in the whole report. A/H pairs come from Eastmoney's AH list; US ADR ↔ HK pairs come from a small CSV in data/ref/.
  - Changed in Phase 5: both come from Eastmoney's company id (ORG_CODE), which is the same for every listing of one issuer (601939 and 00939; BABA and 09988; HSBC and 00005), so no hand-made CSV is needed. The AH list is the blocked push2 API.

When an issuer is picked in two markets, the A-share (the primary listing of an A/H issuer) or the US listing (the China ADRs listed in New York first; most of their HK lines are secondary listings) stays, and the HK pick is swapped for the next HK runner-up the same way as an AI flag, listed in the report as 其他市场已入选.

### 12. Valuation v2 in shadow mode (Phase 8; docs/REVIEW.md A2)
v2 runs after v1's picks in every `run` (stockreport/shadow.py; a failure is logged and never touches v1) and on a saved snapshot with `v2 [--market] [--date]`. Its columns go into the snapshot as `v2_*`; the run prints the v2 lists, the confirmed regime breaks, v1's picks in v2 and v2's answer for every name checks 1-2 excluded. The report stays v1. Modules: history.py (data/history → per-stock features, own P/B, base rates, 业绩预告, revisions) → engine.py (earnings base, history weight, residual income, expected return) → regime.py → anchors.py (peer and own anchors, combination, quality screen, confidence) → shadow.py (buckets, verdicts, printout). Every threshold and weight is a first guess for Phase 9.
- Earnings base: CN 扣非 TTM EPS, replaced by the midpoint of a newer 业绩预告 (扣非 profit, else net profit; year-to-date + last year − the notice's own prior-year figure; 61 CN stocks on 2026-10-18); US less preferred dividends, over book net of preferred equity (latest 10-K shares); HK as reported. Current ROE = median of the trailing ROE and consensus years 2 and 3 on today's book, not years 1-2 as the review says: year 1 mostly overlaps the trailing 12 months, so a one-off in the current year counted twice (Gilead's 2026 in-process R&D charge is in both: −5% current ROE instead of 104%).
- History: the last 10 fiscal years (US continuing operations less preferred dividends; CN weighted ROE × 扣非/EPS; HK ROE_AVG). Weight w = clip((ROE sd / |mean| − 0.3) / 0.7, 0, 1) (1 with 3+ loss years) × cycle factor: 1.0 with 2+ revenue down years or < 5% expected growth, 0.3 for a grower (≤ 1 down year, 15%+ expected growth), 0 for such a grower whose losses all came before the last 3 years (A1: valued on today and consensus), else 0.6. Normal ROE = w × history + (1 − w) × today; below its history it reverts up only after past dips, with no steady 5-year decline (slope < −1.5 points a year, R² > 0.5) and analysts' year-3 ROE at least halfway back; otherwise max(today, min(analysts' year 3, history)), flagged as structural decline.
- Archetypes: financial, cyclical (w ≥ 0.6), growth (15%+ expected and 5-year revenue growth), steady, pre-profit: no profitable history (10-year ROE ≤ 3% or none) and either a loss analysts don't expect to turn or losses in half its years with no positive normal ROE (荣昌生物's licence windfall), plus any stock whose value comes out ≤ 0 without a positive normal ROE. Pre-profit names get no value and no call; a negative book (40 US names: ABBV, MCD...) gets no value either.
- Residual income per share: years 1-3 consensus EPS (3+ analysts; HK year 3 = year 2 × (1 + expected growth ≤ 15%)), pulled toward the ROE path from today to normal by w × 10/25/50%, else that path; years 4-13 ROE fades linearly from w × normal + (1 − w) × year 3's to r + φ × (normal − r), φ = 0.15 + 0.45 × ROE stability + 0.2 × gross margin/60%, in [0.1, 0.8]; book grows at most at the sustainable rate (consensus growth ≤ 15% with 5+ analysts, else half the 5-year revenue growth, else the long-run rate, fading to 4% US / 3% CN, HK; the rest is paid out); terminal = the last excess return growing at that rate with r − g ≥ 3 points.
- Required return: the market's implied return (median expected return of stocks with 3+ analysts and 5+ years of history) + 0.5 × the industry's implied premium (5+ valued members), within −3/+6 points, at least g + 3 (your decision: the backtest decides, default implied). 2026-10-18: CN 7.7% (configured 7.65%), US 7.7% (10.1%), HK 10.5% (10.0%). Expected return = the rate at which the value equals the price (−5% to 40%); implied ROE = the constant ROE over 10 years, with no excess return after, that justifies the price.
- Anchors: peer = log P/B fitted on normal ROE (−10..50%) and expected growth (−10..30%) within the industry with 15+ valued members, else the slopes of the parent sector's fit (US Nasdaq sector, HK first level of the industry type, CN the market: there is no parent in the data) at the industry's own median level (median residual of its 3+ valued members; with 1-2, n/(n+10) of their mean); a negative slope drops that regressor; financials on ROE alone. Own history = median P/B over 7 years (US/HK monthly, 24+ months; CN Eastmoney's half-yearly P/B, 6+) × today's book; the last 3 years after a confirmed break. US: market value / book per month: SEC restates share counts after splits in some years only, so each year's count is moved to today's units by the later splits that bring it closest to the latest count; ADRs and share classes map through latest SEC count / listed units; a share change no split explains (BABA's 2019 ordinary split) or a book jump of 40%+ beyond profit (OMC/IPG merger) or a 40%+ drop in a profitable year (WDC's spin-off) cuts the history there (confidence −1). HK: Eastmoney BPS (CNY, restated like its EPS) at that month's HKDCNY=X against closes in today's units. Combination in logs: (1 − w) × [0.6, 0.3, 0.1] + w × [0.5, 0.1, 0.4] (intrinsic / peer / own), financials [0.55, 0.3, 0.15]; no intrinsic value, no combined value.
- Regime test (A2.7), on the base rates' own episode definition with today as the latest year: current ROE 12+ points above the mean of the last 5 fiscal years, at 18%+ and 1.5× it (down: from 8%+, 12+ points lower and at most half), backed by a net margin 5+ points away from the 5-year margin or revenue growth in the last 3 years 15+ points faster (and 20%+) / slower (and none) than before; a steady grower turning profitable is not a break, and a level that has held for years is the regime (GOOGL's 2021 step). Confirmed when 3+ analysts' year-3 ROE keeps at least half of the move. V_new: consensus without haircut, normal = mean of today and analysts' year 3, φ ≥ 0.5 (down: book follows shrinking revenue); V_old: consensus fully pulled toward the 10-year normal, φ ≤ 0.3. Our probability p = the base rate measured in the market's whole history panel each run (kept ≥ half of the move after 3 years, by direction and cyclical history; 2026-10-18 up-breaks: US 48% of 327, HK 19% of 513, CN 17% of 713) moved in log-odds by the stock's own last boom (±0.5), consensus durability (year 3 / year 1 ≥ 1.15 or < 0.9: ±0.4; a year-2 peak −0.3), a 5-year steady decline for down-breaks (+0.8), the newest 业绩预告 type, revisions (±0.3) and, for AI-tagged stocks, the capex growth of the 10 largest US spenders (+68% on 2026-10-18: +0.3), clipped to 5-95%. The stock is valued at p × V_new + (1 − p) × V_old (one value, one call); the market's implied probability = (price − V_old) / (V_new − V_old). Inventory, backlog and gross-margin direction are not in the tables yet (Phase 9 fits the evidence weights).

### 16. Valuation redesign (Phase 13; drafted 2026-10-19, built 2026-10-20 as valuation v3 in shadow mode)
Why. Your review of the full list (2026-10-19) found results that contradict what is known about the businesses, and the causes are in the method, not the data:
- v1 values most companies by what their peer group pays for their book value (P/B fitted on ROE and one year of revenue growth). Book value is the business for banks and insurers (their results look sensible: US banks mostly within ±25%), but not for software, pharma and brands, whose capital (R&D, people, brands) is expensed: CrowdStrike −88%, Arm −75%.
- Growth enters as one year of past revenue growth and, with 3+ analysts, two years of expected EPS growth. Eli Lilly is priced for a decade of GLP-1 growth; with a two-year window its premium reads as overvaluation (−43%). Industries in structural decline look cheap for the opposite reason.
- Peer groups are industry labels: "Auto Manufacturing" puts Tesla and Ferrari next to Ford and Stellantis, so the legacy makers look like Tesla at a discount (Stellantis +571%, Honda +237%). A group under 8 members is compared with the whole market: Toyota and GM +129%; in CN 269 stocks, a third of the universe, median upside +38% against +3% for stocks compared within their own industry (基础建设 +199%, 乘用车 +92%).
- Relative valuation assumes a stock's distance from what its peers pay is a mistake that will close. Mostly it is information the model can't see (future growth, disruption, quality), so the errors gather exactly where the market holds a strong view.
- The backtest agrees (BACKTEST.md section 3, out of sample 2024-01..2026-09): v1's 12-month rank correlation with later returns is −0.009 in CN (its buys trailed its sells by 4%), +0.076 in the US (plain book-to-price: +0.160), +0.049 in HK. v2 is less extreme (Toyota +36%, Ford −7%) but also misreads long-duration growth (Lilly −28%, CrowdStrike −99%) and does no better in the US.

Principle. A fair value has to come from what the company's own future earnings are worth, with the assumptions written out, and say in one sentence what the price expects: 价格隐含 N 年 g% 的增长；历史上类似的公司做到的比例是 X%. Peers supply base rates and a cross-check, never the level. The market's view is not treated as an error by default: the method shows what the price assumes and how often such an assumption came true. It does not try to forecast the AI cycle or EV adoption; those enter only through analysts' estimates, base rates and the company's own numbers (the deep-dive lane can add verified facts).

Method (built on v2's history, base-rate panel and residual-income code; what it doesn't need is removed):
- One model for every company: value per share = book + the discounted earnings above the cost of equity (residual income), which equals a discounted-cash-flow value when the earnings path is consistent. Years 1-3: analysts' EPS with 3+ analysts (live), else the company's own trend (also the backtest version, which has no consensus history). After year 3, growth and excess returns fade to the market's long-run levels over a duration taken from base rates, not one fixed fade (v2 uses 3 years in CN/HK, 10 in the US).
- Base rates (the outside view), measured on each market's whole history panel every run (US 2015-2025, about 1,000 companies; CN since 2016, the whole market; HK since 2016): of the companies that looked like this one (starting revenue growth band, ROE band, size band, sector), how fast did growth and excess ROE fade over the next 1-5 years? Beyond 5 years the panel is too short: a fixed fade to the long-run level, the same for all. An industry whose companies shrank or lost their excess returns over the panel (traditional carmakers, media) carries that in its base rate, so a low price stays explained unless the company's own numbers break the pattern (v2's regime test).
- Implied expectations: solve for the growth duration that makes the value equal the price, and place it in the base-rate distribution. The fair value range is the value at the 25th / 50th / 75th percentile of that distribution; upside = the 50th / price − 1. A price that needs more than the 90th percentile is 价格隐含过高 (not "overvalued by X%"), less than the 10th 价格隐含过低.
- Book-light companies (negative book after buybacks, or ROE above 40%): valued on the earnings path directly (discounted earnings less the reinvestment the growth needs: value-driver formula with the company's own return on reinvested capital), so Lilly, AbbVie, Apple and McDonald's get values (v2 gives negative-book names none).
- Loss-makers: a value only when analysts (3+) expect a profit within 3 years, from that path; otherwise no fair value, shown as such and never picked (v1's P/S fit on peers goes: it put pre-revenue Roivant at −100%).
- Discount rate: one per market, the market's implied return as Phase 9 calibrated (CN 7.7%, US 7.7%, HK 10.5% on 2026-10-18), the same for every company in a market: risk shows in the range and the base rates, not in a hand-set rate.
- Peers: base rates when the company's own history is short (under 5 years), and a cross-check line (什么同行愿意付 ±Y%), shown, not blended. Groups: the industry; with under 8 members its parent sector (US Nasdaq sector; HK the first level of the industry type; CN 申万 level 1, which the backtest already reads and must be verified for daily use), keeping the industry's own median level; never the whole market. Where a group mixes business models, the base-rate conditioning on growth and ROE does the separating (Tesla and Ferrari don't set Ford's value).
- Confidence: 高 / 中 / 低 from the width of the range relative to the price, years of history, analyst coverage and whether the cross-check agrees; 低信心 as today for simpler or flagged cases.
- Report card line per stock: fair value range, upside, the implied-expectations sentence, the base rate, the peer cross-check.

Built (Phase 13, 2026-10-20; stockreport/baserates.py → v3.py → shadow.py, config `[v3]`, backtest/v3.py). Where the draft left a choice open, the choice and why:
- Base rates (baserates.py), per market, rebuilt every run from what was public that day. A start is a profitable company-year (US: filers with $50m+ revenue); its cell is sector (CN 申万 level 1, US the Fama-French 12 industry of the SEC SIC code, HK the first level of the industry type) × revenue growth band (<0, 0-10, 10-20, 20-35, 35%+) × ROE band (<8, 8-15, 15-25, 25%+) × size (revenue tercile of the year); a cell needs 60 starts, else the next coarser one (without size, then sector × ROE band, then market-wide bands). Two measures per cell:
  - Duration: the share whose profit kept up the stock's own growth rate g for 1..10 years, counted cumulatively (profit summed over the years at least the sum of the g-path, less a 10% tolerance; US 25%): year-on-year profit is so noisy that "every year on the path" ended most runs within 2-3 years even for steady growers. Counted year by year over the companies still reporting (Kaplan-Meier), so a company that stops reporting counts for the years it lived; past the years with 20+ at risk, the last 3 years' hazard continues, to 30 years.
  - ROE path: after the growth years, ROE ≈ A + B × the starting ROE: the cell's level chained year by year with the median change over the companies reporting both years (a cell needs 50 such pairs a year, else no change: short panels made noise), B the share of a company's distance from the cell's mean still there k years later. This carries an industry's drift (CN construction and banks kept falling).
- Value (v3.py): residual income. ### 17. Picking by funnel and score (drafted 2026-10-20, waiting for your review; nothing built)

Your target (2026-10-20): a buy beats the market over 6 months without a big blow-up (12 months is too much to ask). Your idea: replace one layer of filtering plus a fair-value rank by a funnel (filters with different purposes that narrow the field step by step) and a final rank on a weighted score (value, risk, confidence, business attractiveness), because ranking on fair value alone puts the stocks the method values worst at the top.

**Definitions (proposed; your call).** The market: each market's index (S&P 500, CSI 300, Hang Seng); stock returns include dividends and the indexes don't, which makes the bar 1-2 points easier over 6 months. A blow-up: the stock 25% or more below its start at any month-end within the 6 months. Sells mirror buys: they trail the index over 6 months. Holds: neither.

**Where we start** (scratch measurement on the backtest frames, 6-month returns; out of sample 2024-01..2026-03, 27 dates; in-sample from v3's first valued month, US 2019-02, CN 2019-01, HK 2019-06):

| 6 months | US | CN | HK |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's v3 buys beat the index: out (in) | 69% (48%) | 53% (51%) | 63% (65%) |
| their blow-ups | 3.7% (11.2%) | 6.7% (8.3%) | 3.0% (12.0%) |
| all stocks: beat the index / blow-ups, out | 46% / 7.8% | 42% / 11.6% | 44% / 13.3% |
| v1 buys beat the index: out (in) | 50% (46%) | 38% (50%) | 50% (55%) |

Today's v3 lists are already a small funnel (data errors out, the quality screen, confidence 高/中, a trend rule, at most 2 per industry) and roughly meet the target in HK and in the US out of sample; CN is borderline and the US in-sample is below half. The new design has to beat these lists, not only v1.

**The funnel** (each stage defined by its purpose before any test and applied to every stock; not a rule added after seeing one bad name):
1. Data we can trust: cross-checks and 数据错误 out (built).
2. Investable: size and trading volume (built; cut-offs reviewed per market).
3. Blow-up risk: first measure which observable signs preceded 25%+ falls within 6 months in each market (volatility, debt, losses, falling estimates, price trend, CN speculative heat); the stage then sets aside the names whose measured blow-up chance is over twice the market's, a cut declared before the test.
4. A business worth valuing: the quality screen (built).
5. The score: valuation signal (v3's gap as a rank within the market, scaled down by confidence only if confidence proves to predict accuracy), 6-month price trend, business quality and health (one input, overlaps removed), consensus revisions (live only), risk (volatility). Sells: the mirror, among the largest names. Two or three weightings declared in advance per market (equal; valuation-led; risk-led); in-sample picks one, out of sample confirms it once.

**Checks before the score is built** (to make it reliable, your question 2026-10-20):
- 6-month returns and blow-ups added to the backtest for all three markets.
- Each input on its own: does it raise the 6-month win rate and lower blow-ups, in both samples and every market?
- Confidence calibrated: do 高-confidence gaps close more than 低 ones? If not, it leaves the score.
- Overlaps measured (health contains momentum and ROE, quality contains ROE, confidence contains the gap size); duplicates merged or dropped.
- Biases measured: survivorship (the US and HK backtests hold today's listed companies only; CN is point-in-time and serves as the check), trading costs and turnover.

**Pass criteria (registered now, before any run).** Per market, out of sample: buys beat the index at least 55% of the time with blow-ups at most half the market's rate, and at least as good as today's v3 lists on both; the same direction in-sample; over all stocks, the top fifth by score beats the bottom fifth on 6-month return and blow-ups in both samples; it holds in at least 3 of 4 sub-periods (2019-20, 2021-22, 2023, 2024-26) and in at least two markets; results shown with ranges (resampled by date), and five-name lists shown but not used to choose; the spot-check panel (the 25 hand-checked names plus your examples) reads sensibly, and no pick rests on a gap beyond ±100% alone.

**What it doesn't change.** v3's values and the card's fair-value number (a Phase 10 question); the report stays v1; v3 stays in shadow. The app stays under 6,000 lines (5,976 now).

Concepts are merged per period in a preference order because companies switch tags between filings, with three rules from the Phase 12 cross-checks (2026-10-19), also in the annual history: revenue is chosen per period, because companies also tag parts of it with its concepts (ASC 606 fee revenue at M&T, AmEx and SoFi, Blue Owl's "Revenues", Zions' non-interest revenue subtotal) and totals before intersegment eliminations (Williams' ASC 606 total, Hasbro's "Revenues"): the concept within 5% of what the income statement implies (gross profit + cost of revenue, else operating income + total costs and expenses), else the largest concept (REITs' rent: OperatingLeaseLeaseIncome), and for banks net interest + noninterest income when larger; a 12-month figure comes only from a 10-K (Comfort Systems' Q1 2026 10-Q tagged Q1 2025 revenue as the 2025 year); equity and net income are the parent's: the total including minorities less MinorityInterest when only the total is tagged (AT&T, Carlyle, TPG, Royalty Pharma), ProfitLoss less the minorities' share when NetIncomeLoss is missing (Invesco).
